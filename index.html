Key Findings

Extreme Weather Is Becoming More Frequent

Beyond rising averages, the number of extreme hot days and heavy-rainfall days each year is also trending up.

Proof: Predicting the Future, Then Checking Against Reality

Every model here was trained only on data through 31 December 2025. We then forecast the first week of January 2026 — and compared it against what actually happened, using real observed data collected afterward.

About This Analysis

Data: ERA5 Reanalysis, Copernicus Climate Data Store, 1980–2025 (46 years), daily temperature and rainfall at ~28km resolution. Trend detection: Mann-Kendall test with Sen's slope estimator, cross-checked with BEAST Bayesian changepoint analysis. Prediction: LSTM neural network and Random Forest, compared against a naive baseline. Explainability: SHAP values used to identify what actually drives extreme-event predictions.

This is a research project, not an official weather or climate forecasting service. Projections shown are statistical trend continuations, not day-by-day weather forecasts.
